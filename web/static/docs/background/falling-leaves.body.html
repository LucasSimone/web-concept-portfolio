Falling Leaves

A canvas of leaves falling through an open field. Each one swings side to side on its own pendulum: it drops fastest through the middle of a swing, slows to about a third of that speed at either end, and tilts into the direction it is swinging. It also turns over about its midrib, showing a darker underside when it is past edge-on. Leaves sit at different depths, and farther ones are drawn smaller, slower and fainter. A steady wind leans the whole fall to one side. Every few seconds a gust builds, rolls across from the upwind edge, makes the leaves it reaches tumble faster and lifts them slightly, then dies away. Nothing responds to the cursor. Purely decorative — it sits behind the element's content and never touches real page elements.

Install

<script src="falling-leaves.js"></script>

Injects its own <style> tag positioning the canvas behind the element's content, so no companion CSS file is required to render it — but the host element needs its own height/content, since the injected CSS doesn't size it.

Quick Start

<section class="bg-falling-leaves" aria-label="Falling leaves background">
  <span class="label">Drift and turn</span>
</section>
<script src="falling-leaves.js"></script>

No data attribute needed — just apply the class to a container with a size of its own. A ResizeObserver on the host element keeps the canvas sized to fit, clamping any leaves that would otherwise fall outside the new bounds rather than restarting the whole fall. Auto-initializes against every .bg-falling-leaves element. To configure options: 

FallingLeaves.initAll('.bg-falling-leaves', {
  count: 60,
  size: 18,
});

Recipes

Windy day

A strong steady wind, harder gusts and faster tumbling. Lower sway keeps the wind in charge instead of each leaf's own swing. 

FallingLeaves.initAll('.bg-falling-leaves', {
  count: 70,
  fallSpeed: 70,
  sway: 25,
  tumble: 1.8,
  wind: 60,
  gusts: 2,
});

Still afternoon

No wind and no gusts, a slow fall and wide swings, so a few leaves rock their way down instead of being blown. 

FallingLeaves.initAll('.bg-falling-leaves', {
  count: 15,
  fallSpeed: 20,
  sway: 55,
  tumble: 0.5,
  wind: 0,
  gusts: 0,
});

A range of colors

With blend on, colors is read as a gradient through the colors in the order given, instead of a list to pick from. Each leaf lands somewhere along it, so two colors give every shade from one to the other, and a third becomes a stop in the middle. 

FallingLeaves.initAll('.bg-falling-leaves', {
  colors: ['#f2c14e', '#d3622a', '#7a1f12'],
  blend: true,
});

Blossom petals

The built-in 'petal' shape over a pale-to-deep pink range, small and plentiful. Petals get no midrib line, unlike the leaf shapes. 

FallingLeaves.initAll('.bg-falling-leaves', {
  shapes: ['petal'],
  colors: ['#fbe1e6', '#e98aa2'],
  blend: true,
  count: 90,
  size: 7,
  fallSpeed: 25,
});

Over an existing backdrop

Set background to 'transparent' (or null) and the canvas is cleared each frame instead of filled, so leaves fall over whatever the host element already shows: a photo, a gradient, a dark theme. The injected styles give the host a white background and are added at the end of <head>, so override it with a more specific selector, such as .hero.bg-falling-leaves. 

FallingLeaves.initAll('.bg-falling-leaves', {
  background: 'transparent',
});

Custom Shapes

shapes is a list, and each leaf picks one entry from it at random. An entry can be the name of a built-in shape (FallingLeaves.SHAPES lists them: 'maple', 'oak', 'birch', 'oval', 'petal'), raw SVG path data, or a ready-made Path2D. Draw your own in a box from -1 to 1 on both axes, centered on 0, 0: that is the point the leaf spins and flips around, and size is how far 1 unit reaches, in px. Custom shapes are drawn without the midrib line the built-in leaves get. 

// Confetti: a mix of squares and thin strips, in bright colors.
FallingLeaves.initAll('.bg-falling-leaves', {
  shapes: [
    'M-0.6 -0.6 H0.6 V0.6 H-0.6 Z',
    'M-0.25 -1 H0.25 V1 H-0.25 Z',
  ],
  colors: ['#e63946', '#f4a261', '#2a9d8f', '#457b9d', '#ffd166'],
  tumble: 2.5,
  sway: 20,
});

Mixing built-in names and your own paths in one list works too, as does repeating an entry to make it more common.

Options

OptionDefaultDescription
count40How many leaves are falling at once. A leaf that leaves through the bottom comes back in at the top
fallSpeed45Fall speed of the nearest leaves through the middle of a swing, in px/sec. Each leaf varies it by ±20%, it drops to about a third at either end of a swing, and farther leaves fall more slowly
sway40How far the nearest leaves swing either side of their path, in px. Farther leaves swing less. 0 falls straight down
tumble1Multiplier on every leaf's own flip and spin rate. 0 holds every leaf at its starting orientation, apart from the tilt of its swing, so some stay underside-up and a few edge-on
wind10Steady sideways drift in px/sec; negative blows left. Also sets which way gusts blow (random when 0). The farthest leaves feel about 80% of it
gusts1Strength multiplier for the occasional gusts, each peaking at 35–70 px/sec before this is applied. 0 turns them off
size14Half a near leaf's length, in px (1 unit of the shape's -1..1 box). Each leaf varies it by ±25%, and farther leaves are drawn smaller
background'#ffffff'Canvas fill drawn behind the leaves each frame. 'transparent' or null skips the fill
colorsfive autumn hexesArray of CSS colors (hex, rgb(), named, anything the canvas accepts; transparency is ignored). Each leaf picks one (or a point between them, with blend) and shifts its lightness slightly. The underside and midrib are darker shades of the same color
blendfalseWhen true, colors is a gradient through its colors in order, spaced evenly, and each leaf lands anywhere along it. When false, each leaf is exactly one of the listed colors
shapes['maple', 'oak', 'birch', 'oval']Array of built-in shape names, SVG path data strings, or Path2D objects; each leaf picks one. See Custom Shapes

Methods

MethodDescription
.update(options)Merges new options in. Changing count adds or removes leaves immediately; changing colors, blend or shapes repaints or reshapes every leaf already falling (each leaf keeps its own place in the palette, so editing a color changes the leaves drawn from it rather than reshuffling the whole field); anything else applies on the next frame, including to a gust already blowing.
.destroy()Stops the animation loop, disconnects the resize and visibility observers, and removes the canvas.
FallingLeaves.get(elOrSelector) / FallingLeaves.getAll(selector)Static lookups for already-initialized instances, by element/selector.
FallingLeaves.SHAPESThe names of the built-in shapes.

Notes

Gust timing is fixed rather than configurable: one builds over about 1.2 seconds, takes about 0.9 seconds to roll from one edge to the other, dies away over 3 seconds, and the next arrives 3 to 9 seconds after that. Leaves drift off one side and back in from the other rather than piling up against the edge. The loop pauses when the element is scrolled well out of view. Respects prefers-reduced-motion by skipping the animation loop entirely and drawing one still frame of leaves where they were spawned, so the field is still visible but doesn't move. The demo page edits colors and blend directly, up to six colors; shapes only changes with its Profile dropdown, so reach for that option in code for your own shapes.
