Flip Dots

A programmable grid of physical discs. Each one sits on a center axle: asked for a new state it turns a half turn, slams into its magnet stop, and the color swaps at the exact moment it passes edge-on — which is why a disc with two faces can hold any number of colors. Hover the board to flip single dots; click one to send a ripple out from it.

Words, not just pixels

A board that says things

The same component with a 5×7 bitmap font on top of it. A message is just another whole-board update, so it arrives on a transition like anything else — and because only the dots that actually differ between two messages flip, changing one word to another costs the handful of discs that disagree rather than the whole board.

Any size

Same component, three scales

The grid is measured against the element's own box. Give it a dotSize and the columns follow; give it cols and the dot size follows. Below about five pixels a disc has no room to read as a disc, so a grid asked for finer than that is thinned rather than drawn too small — each board below reports what it actually got.

A live display

Driven by data

A clock, redrawn every second. Only the dots whose state actually differs flip, so a tick costs the handful of discs that make up the digit that changed — watch the minute roll over and the rest of the board stays perfectly still.

No transition on a tick: a clock that rippled every second would be a toy rather than a clock.

As a surface

Content sits on top

The canvas inserts itself as the element’s first child, behind everything else in it, so a board can carry its own content instead of only being a display. This whole section is one instance — the heading and this paragraph are its children. Holding the lit state well down from full brightness keeps the text the brightest thing on it.
